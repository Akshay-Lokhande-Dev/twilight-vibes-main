Twenty-eight years after two witch sisters made "midnight margaritas" a permanent part of pop culture, the Owens women are coming home. Practical Magic 2 hits theaters on September 11, 2026, and unlike a lot of legacy sequels that lean entirely on nostalgia, this one actually has a story to tell.

Here's everything confirmed so far — no filler, no guessing.

The Setup: 25 Years Later, Same Curse

The sequel picks up a quarter-century after the original. Sally (Sandra Bullock) has settled into exactly the quiet life you'd expect from someone who's spent decades trying to outrun a family curse — she's puttering in a vegetable garden, baking elaborate cakes, and doing her best not to think about magic at all. Gillian (Nicole Kidman), true to form, is still Gillian: restless, a little reckless, currently living with just a black cat for company.

The curse itself hasn't gone anywhere. If you remember the original film, the Owens women are doomed — any man who falls in love with one of them dies. Sally spent the first movie refusing to teach her daughters magic specifically to protect them from that fate. That decision is what the sequel is built around.

Sally's Daughters Are All Grown Up

This is the actual engine of the plot, and it's worth understanding because it's the emotional core the marketing hasn't fully spelled out.

Sally's daughters — Kylie (Joey King) and Antonia (Maisie Williams) — are now adults, and they're dealing with their own magical abilities despite Sally's efforts to keep them away from the craft entirely. The trailer shows Kylie attempting to break the family curse herself, after her boyfriend (played by Xolo Maridueña) gets caught in its crosshairs. That choice sends her down what the studio is calling a "dangerous and potentially dark path."

Which means Sally and Gillian have to do the one thing neither of them wanted to do again: dig back into the family's magic, this time to save the next generation instead of themselves.

There's a structural parallel here worth noting for anyone who's watched a lot of multi-generational fantasy stories — the mother who ran from her power spends the sequel watching her daughter walk straight toward the same danger she spent 25 years avoiding. It's a familiar shape, but it's one that tends to land hard when the actors are this good.

Cast: New Faces & Returning Favorites

Joining the cast is Lee Pace as Ian Wright, described as a scholar of magical history who shows up in the Owens' small New England town with an agenda. He ends up helping Sally unlock powers she didn't know she had — and becomes her love interest, which, given the whole "loving an Owens woman is fatal" thing, is not exactly a low-stakes development.

Where the Story Actually Goes

Part of what's different this time: the sisters and aunts reportedly travel from Massachusetts to the United Kingdom to confront the actual origins of the family's magic. The original film never really dug into why the Owens women are cursed — this sequel seems built to answer that.

It's based on Alice Hoffman's 2021 novel The Book of Magic, the third book in her Practical Magic series, which gives the film more source material to draw from than the first movie had.

Why This Matters for Fans of Cursed Love Stories

If you grew up on stories where loving someone means risking everything — and if that's the emotional register that pulled you into things like Twilight in the first place — Practical Magic 2 is working the same territory from a different angle. Instead of an outside threat (a vampire's world, a werewolf pack), the danger here is inherited. It's a curse baked into the bloodline, passed down like a family trait nobody asked for.

Both stories, in their own ways, are about deciding whether love is worth the risk when the risk is real and specific, not hypothetical. That's probably why the original Practical Magic — despite a rocky box office run and mixed reviews in 1998 — became the kind of cult classic people rewatch every October, cardigan in hand, margarita in the other.

Is Practical Magic 2 connected to the Twilight universe?

No — they're entirely separate franchises with no shared characters or continuity. The comparison is thematic (cursed love, generational danger), not literal.

Do you need to watch the original Practical Magic first?

Given the sequel picks up 25 years later and leans on the emotional history between Sally and Gillian, yes — it'll land much harder if you've seen the 1998 original first.

It draws from The Book of Magic, the third novel in Alice Hoffman's Practical Magic book series, published in 2021.

Is Evan Rachel Wood in Practical Magic 2?

No. Wood played Sally's daughter Kylie in the 1998 original, but she confirmed on social media that she wasn't asked back — even offering to appear in just one scene. The role is being recast, with Joey King and Maisie Williams playing Sally's daughters in the sequel instead.

Does someone die to break the curse in Practical Magic 2?

We don't know yet — Warner Bros. hasn't confirmed plot details that specific. But curse-breaking stories like this one usually come with a cost, and fans have already been speculating about a possible sacrifice since the trailer dropped. We'll update this once the film is out.

Who is in the cast of Practical Magic 2?

Sandra Bullock and Nicole Kidman return as Sally and Gillian Owens, alongside Joey King and Maisie Williams as Sally's daughters, Lee Pace as new character Ian Wright, and returning cast Stockard Channing and Dianne Wiest as the aunts.
