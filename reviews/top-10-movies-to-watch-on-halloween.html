Top 10 Movies to Watch on Halloween Night

Ten movies that feel like October 31st, whether you want 90s comfort witches or a reason to leave the hall light on.

🎃 Picked by TwilightVibes · 2026 Edition

By TwilightVibes · October 16, 2026

In this watchlist
  1. Halloween (1978): old-school slasher tension
  2. Trick 'r Treat (2007): Halloween night rules
  3. Scream (1996): best for a loud group
  4. Hocus Pocus (1993): 90s Salem nostalgia
  5. Hereditary (2018): dread that sticks around
  6. The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993): the bridge to November
  7. The Conjuring (2013): classic haunted house
  8. Beetlejuice (1988): weird, practical-effects comedy

Every year we lose about forty-five minutes of Halloween night to the streaming scroll. The pizza is open, the porch light is on for trick-or-treaters, and nobody on the couch can agree on a movie. Half the room wants something brutal. The other half just wants autumn leaves and no panic attack.

We're picky about what counts as a Halloween movie. If you've read our Twilight Universe Guide, you know we like a grey Pacific Northwest autumn, but October 31st has its own rules. A horror movie set on a sunny beach in July doesn't count. We want jack-o'-lanterns on porches, dead leaves in the street, or a ghost story that earns its scares.

Here are our top 10 movies to watch on Halloween, sorted by the kind of night you're having.

1. Halloween (1978)

Pick #1 · The Original Slasher
Halloween (1978)

Director: John Carpenter · Starring: Jamie Lee Curtis, Donald Pleasence, Nick Castle · Runtime: 91 mins · Rating: R

Best for: Lights off, when you want slow suspense instead of gore.

You can't make a Halloween list without John Carpenter's original. Some younger viewers call it slow, because after the opening the movie takes its time before Michael starts killing again, and there is very little blood overall. That patience is what makes it work.

The story is simple. In 1963, six-year-old Michael Myers murders his older sister on Halloween night. Fifteen years later he escapes a transfer from Smith's Grove Sanitarium, steals a car, and heads home to Haddonfield, Illinois, where he starts following a high school babysitter named Laurie Strode. It was Jamie Lee Curtis's first film role. Later sequels tried to explain Michael with curses and cults, but here he's just a man in coveralls standing behind a hedge. (If slasher history is your thing, we also covered the Crystal Lake prequel series.)

Made for $300,000, shot in spring

Carpenter and producer Debra Hill made the film for roughly $300,000 and shot it in about three weeks in South Pasadena, California, in the spring of 1978. The trees were green, so the crew scattered fake autumn leaves on the street and raked them up between takes.

The mask was just as cheap. The crew bought a Captain Kirk mask from a Hollywood shop for under two dollars, widened the eyeholes, removed the hair and painted it white. Add Carpenter's piano theme in an odd 5/4 rhythm and you have a template that horror still copies.

2. Trick 'r Treat (2007)

Pick #2 · The Holiday Anthology
Trick 'r Treat (2007)

Director: Michael Dougherty · Starring: Dylan Baker, Anna Paquin, Brian Cox, Quinn Lord · Runtime: 82 mins · Rating: R

Best for: Jack-o'-lanterns, costumes and dark comedy in every scene.

Carpenter uses Halloween as a backdrop. Trick 'r Treat treats it like law. Four stories overlap in one Ohio town on a single night. A friendly school principal (Dylan Baker, clearly enjoying himself) poisons trick-or-treaters and buries bodies in his yard. A group of kids go to a quarry to pull a prank tied to the local legend of the 1986 school bus tragedy. Anna Paquin plays a shy young woman in a Red Riding Hood costume looking for a date, and Brian Cox is a grumpy neighbor who hates the holiday.

The movie has a strange release history. Warner Bros. shelved it after a festival run in 2007 and it went straight to DVD in 2009. Horror fans did the rest. The star is Sam, a small figure in orange pajamas and a burlap sack mask who only punishes people who break the rules: keep your jack-o'-lantern lit until midnight, hand out candy, wear a costume, check your sweets. At 82 minutes, nothing is wasted.

3. Scream (1996)

Pick #3 · The 90s Crowd-Pleaser
Scream (1996)

Director: Wes Craven · Starring: Neve Campbell, Courteney Cox, David Arquette, Skeet Ulrich, Matthew Lillard, Drew Barrymore · Runtime: 111 mins · Rating: R

Best for: Group watch parties where everyone guesses the killer and yells at the screen.

By the mid 90s the slasher genre was mostly direct-to-video leftovers. Screenwriter Kevin Williamson wrote a script, first titled Scary Movie, where the teenagers in Woodsboro have seen all the same horror films we have and know the rules. He has said the idea came partly from the real Gainesville murders. The film opened on December 20, 1996, but it works just as well in October, especially with four or five friends in the room.

The opening scene

Drew Barrymore was originally cast as Sidney, then asked to play Casey Becker instead. The idea was that if the biggest name on the poster could die in the first twelve minutes, nobody else was safe. Roger L. Jackson, who voices Ghostface, spoke her lines on the phone live from off set, so she never saw him. Matthew Lillard improvised his famous "my mom and dad are gonna be so mad at me" line in the final act.

4. Hocus Pocus (1993)

Pick #4 · The Comfort Rewatch
Hocus Pocus (1993)

Director: Kenny Ortega · Starring: Bette Midler, Sarah Jessica Parker, Kathy Najimy, Omri Katz, Thora Birch · Runtime: 96 mins · Rating: PG

Best for: Early evening, while you hand out candy.

Not everyone wants a masked killer on Halloween. If you grew up in the 90s or 2000s, October starts when Max moves from Los Angeles to Salem, lights the Black Flame Candle to impress a girl, and brings three witches back from the dead. Bette Midler, Sarah Jessica Parker and Kathy Najimy play the Sanderson sisters with no restraint at all, and their "I Put a Spell on You" number is still the best scene. (If witches and magic schools are your thing, see our Coven Academy (2026) preview.)

It's odd that Disney released it in July 1993, up against Jurassic Park. It made about $39 million on a $28 million budget and was seen as a disappointment. It found its audience through VHS and years of October TV airings.

5. Hereditary (2018)

Pick #5 · Heavy Psychological Dread
Hereditary (2018)

Director: Ari Aster · Starring: Toni Collette, Alex Wolff, Milly Shapiro, Gabriel Byrne · Runtime: 127 mins · Rating: R

Best for: People who say horror movies don't scare them anymore.

Ari Aster's debut is the opposite of a cozy watch. A24 released it in June 2018 after Sundance, and it earned around $80 million worldwide, which made it one of the studio's biggest hits at the time. (We also covered another A24 project in our Backrooms movie explainer.)

It starts with a funeral for the mother of Annie Graham (Toni Collette), a miniature artist. The family tries to move on in their isolated house, then a terrible tragedy hits. What looks like a drama about grief and resentment between Annie and her teenage son Peter (Alex Wolff) slowly turns into something much darker. Be warned: this is not a movie to put on if you want to sleep well afterward.

The house

Because Annie builds tiny models of her own life, the filmmakers built the Graham house as a set rather than shooting in a real home. The camera moves through rooms in a way that feels like looking into one of her dioramas.

6. The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993)

Pick #6 · The Midnight Transition
The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993)

Director: Henry Selick · Starring: Danny Elfman, Chris Sarandon, Catherine O'Hara, Ken Page · Runtime: 76 mins · Rating: PG

Best for: Around 11 PM, as Halloween turns into November.

People argue every year about whether this is a Halloween or a Christmas movie. The first song is "This Is Halloween," the story opens on Halloween night in Halloween Town, and at 76 minutes it's the shortest watch on the list. Jack Skellington, the Pumpkin King, is tired of the same routine. He stumbles into Christmas Town and decides Halloween Town should take over December 25th.

Tim Burton wrote the original poem and produced the film, but Henry Selick directed it. Jack had hundreds of replacement heads, with more than 400 faces for his expressions, and animators finished roughly a minute of footage a week. Disney also released it under the Touchstone label at first, because they worried it was too dark for the main brand.

7. The Conjuring (2013)

Pick #7 · Haunted House Rollercoaster
The Conjuring (2013)

Director: James Wan · Starring: Vera Farmiga, Patrick Wilson, Lili Taylor, Ron Livingston · Runtime: 112 mins · Rating: R

Best for: Jump scares that get the whole living room screaming.

Sometimes you just want a haunted house. James Wan's film is still the best of the Conjuring series, and it turned a roughly $20 million budget into about $319 million worldwide. It's set in 1971, when the Perron family moves into a Rhode Island farmhouse. The dog won't go inside, the clocks stop at 3:07 AM, there's a smell of rot, and Carolyn keeps waking up with bruises. She turns to paranormal investigators Ed and Lorraine Warren (Patrick Wilson and Vera Farmiga).

Why it's rated R

There's almost no gore, no sex and little swearing. The MPAA still gave it an R for "disturbing violence and terror." The ratings board judged it too intense for PG-13 on atmosphere alone. The hide-and-clap scene shows why.

8. Beetlejuice (1988)

Pick #8 · Practical-Effects Chaos
Beetlejuice (1988)

Director: Tim Burton · Starring: Michael Keaton, Winona Ryder, Geena Davis, Alec Baldwin, Catherine O'Hara · Runtime: 92 mins · Rating: PG

Best for: Gothic comedy, handmade effects and Harry Belafonte on the soundtrack.

Tim Burton's second feature turns the haunted house around. Adam and Barbara Maitland (Alec Baldwin and Geena Davis) die in a car accident and end up stuck in their own Connecticut home as ghosts. When the pretentious Deetz family moves in, the Maitlands are too polite to scare them off, so they hire Betelgeuse (Michael Keaton), a sleazy "bio-exorcist."

Keaton is only on screen for around 17 minutes of the 92, and he steals the movie anyway. The film won the Oscar for Best Makeup. The dinner party where the guests are forced to sing and dance to "Day-O" is the highlight.

9. Practical Magic (1998)

Pick #9 · Autumn Sisterhood
Practical Magic (1998)

Director: Griffin Dunne · Starring: Sandra Bullock, Nicole Kidman, Stockard Channing, Dianne Wiest · Runtime: 104 mins · Rating: PG-13

Best for: Big sweaters, Stevie Nicks on the soundtrack and midnight margaritas.

Based on Alice Hoffman's 1995 novel, Practical Magic got mixed reviews in 1998, partly because it moves from romantic comedy to domestic abuse and a kitchen exorcism. Audiences have warmed to it since. Sandra Bullock and Nicole Kidman play sisters Sally and Gillian Owens, raised by their eccentric aunts (Stockard Channing and Dianne Wiest) in a coastal Massachusetts town, under a family curse: any man an Owens woman loves will die young. When Gillian's violent boyfriend dies, the sisters try forbidden magic to fix it, and things get worse.

Want more? Read our guide to Practical Magic 2 once you've finished your rewatch.

The house

The Owens house is the real star for many fans. Sadly you can't visit it. The exterior was built on San Juan Island in Washington specifically for the shoot, and the interiors were done on a soundstage.

10. Coraline (2009)

Pick #10 · Stop-Motion Folk Horror
Coraline (2009)

Director: Henry Selick · Starring: Dakota Fanning, Teri Hatcher, Keith David, Ian McShane · Runtime: 100 mins · Rating: PG

Best for: Rainy Pacific Northwest mood and a fairytale that turns creepy.

Selick gets a second spot with Laika's first feature, adapted from Neil Gaiman's novella. The PG rating is misleading. After moving into an old house in Oregon, Coraline finds a small door that leads to an "Other World," where her Other Mother cooks her favorite meals, listens to her, and has buttons for eyes. Then she offers Coraline a pair of buttons of her own.

The handmade detail is the best part. Laika's team even had tiny sweaters knitted for the puppets, and the Other World slowly fading to white as the Other Mother drops her disguise is the scariest moment in the film.

Halloween double feature pairings

If you have four hours tonight, these pairs work well back to back:

The October 31st rules
Hocus Pocus + Trick 'r Treat

Start with Hocus Pocus around 7 PM while kids are still at the door, then switch to Trick 'r Treat once the porch light goes off.

Late-90s time capsule
Practical Magic + Scream

Start with Practical Magic while you make drinks, then put on Scream when the food arrives.

The Selick stop-motion
Coraline + Nightmare Before Christmas

Together they run under three hours, and both are handmade stop-motion.

Lights off past midnight
The Conjuring + Hereditary

Haunted farmhouse first, family nightmare second. Keep a lamp nearby.

FAQ: Halloween movie watchlist

What is the best movie to put on for actual Halloween night?

John Carpenter's Halloween (1978) and Michael Dougherty's Trick 'r Treat (2007) are the top two. Both are set on October 31st and make the holiday itself the main event.

What can I watch on Halloween if I hate gore?

Try Hocus Pocus (1993), Beetlejuice (1988), Practical Magic (1998), or The Nightmare Before Christmas (1993). All four have the autumn mood, witches and ghosts without heavy blood or slasher violence.

Why is The Conjuring rated R if there isn't much gore?

The MPAA gave The Conjuring (2013) an R rating for "sequences of disturbing violence and terror." There is very little blood or profanity. The rating was about how intense the film is.

Is Coraline too scary for younger kids?

It can be. It is rated PG, but the last act has a button-eyed mother figure who wants to sew buttons over a child's eyes. Kids around 9 and up usually enjoy it, while younger kids often find it frightening.

Comfort rewatch or lights-out horror tonight? Happy Halloween from TwilightVibes. Don't blow out your jack-o'-lantern before midnight.
