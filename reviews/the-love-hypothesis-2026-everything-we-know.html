If you've spent any time on BookTok in the last few years, you already know exactly what "Dr. Adam Carlsen" means to a very specific, very loud corner of the internet. Ali Hazelwood's The Love Hypothesis has been one of the biggest romance debuts of the decade, and it's finally getting the screen adaptation everyone's been begging for since 2021.

What Is The Love Hypothesis About?

Olive Smith is a PhD candidate who's perfectly happy keeping science, not romance, at the center of her life. That plan falls apart the moment she needs to convince her best friend Anh that she's over her crush — so she does the first thing that comes to mind and kisses a total stranger in the hallway. That stranger turns out to be Dr. Adam Carlsen, one of the most intimidating professors on campus.

What starts as a panicked cover story turns into a fake relationship of convenience: Adam's research funding is at risk unless the department believes he's not a "flight risk," and a serious girlfriend might change their minds. Two people who are both very good at running experiments end up running one on each other, with predictably messy results for both of their carefully constructed theories about love.

Wait, This Started as Star Wars Fanfiction?

Yes, genuinely. Before it was a bestselling novel, The Love Hypothesis began life in 2018 as a piece of Reylo fanfiction — the Rey and Kylo Ren pairing from the newer Star Wars films — titled "Head Over Feet." Hazelwood later reworked the story into an original contemporary romance with new characters, but she's kept the connection: the male lead is named Adam as a direct nod to Adam Driver, who played Kylo Ren.

Here's the part that made the internet lose its mind when casting was announced: Tom Bateman, the actor now playing Adam Carlsen, is married to Daisy Ridley — the actress who played Rey. Some book fans were initially disappointed that Adam Driver himself wasn't cast, but once that detail got out, most came around fast. The movie's Adam is, in a roundabout way, married to the actress who inspired the whole story in the first place.

It's a genuinely fun piece of trivia, and also a pretty good example of how much of modern romance publishing has fanfiction roots hiding in plain sight.

Book vs. Movie: What Fans Are Watching For

Whenever a beloved book gets the screen treatment, readers show up with a very specific checklist. For this one, the two biggest worries were the casting and the rating — and both seem to have landed well.

On casting: once fans learned Bateman was married to Ridley, the "why isn't Adam Driver playing Adam" grumbling mostly turned into delight at the accidental full-circle joke. On rating: romance adaptations have a rough track record of sanding down the "spicier" material from their source novels, and fans were bracing for the same here. Instead, the film landed an R rating, which read as a genuine signal that the studio wasn't planning to soften the slow-burn tension that made the book work in the first place.

Early test-screening reactions have also been reassuring. Several book influencers and critics who caught early screenings have described it as a faithful adaptation that captures the "beautifully awkward" dynamic between Olive and Adam, for whatever early word-of-mouth is worth ahead of the actual release.

Who Is Ali Hazelwood?

Ali Hazelwood is a pen name — the woman behind it is an Italian-born neuroscience professor who moved to the US for her PhD and now works in academia while writing romance on the side. She's lived in Japan and Germany before settling in the States, and by her own account splits her free time between running, knitting, and her cats.

The Love Hypothesis was her 2021 debut, and it kicked off a run of what BookTok affectionately calls "STEMinist" romance — novels built around women in male-dominated academic and scientific fields, usually paired with a tall, broody love interest who's secretly obsessed with them. Her catalog since then includes Love on the Brain (2022), Love, Theoretically (2023), and Check & Mate (2023, a chess-themed departure from the lab-coat setting), along with the paranormal romance Bride. Her academic background isn't just window dressing — she's written that she's drawing directly on her own experience as one of very few women in her PhD cohort.

Is It Faithful to the Book?

Early signs are good. Book readers picked apart the trailer frame by frame and found several scenes lifted straight from the novel, including the moment that kicks off the whole fake-dating plan and a hotel-room beat that a lot of fans consider the emotional turning point of the story. With Hazelwood on board as an executive producer, the adaptation has a built-in reason to stay close to the source material rather than reinvent it.

Why This Matters for Twilight Fans

The "grumpy professor, sunshine student" dynamic here is really just a modern academia-flavored spin on the same tension that made Bella and Edward work — the slow-burn push and pull of two people who are clearly into each other and clearly in denial about it. If fake-dating tropes and reluctant chemistry are your thing, this one scratches a similar itch, minus the vampires and plus a lot more lab coats.
