If your algorithm has been feeding you nothing but Bridgerton edits and Pride and Prejudice re-reads lately (same), you're going to want to sit down for this one. Focus Features is bringing Jane Austen's Dashwood sisters back to the big screen, and the cast alone has period-drama girlies already refreshing Letterboxd for release day.

This isn't a Twilight-adjacent pick in the vampire sense, but if you're here for the slow-burn longing, the letters that never get sent, and someone getting their heart quietly wrecked in a drawing room — you already know this is exactly our kind of chaos.

What Is It About?

The story picks up right after the Dashwood sisters lose everything — their father dies, the family money goes to a half-brother, and Elinor, Marianne, their younger sister Margaret, and their mother end up starting over in a small rented cottage far from the life they knew. From there, both older sisters fall for men who complicate things in very different ways: one quietly, one catastrophically.

Elinor (Daisy Edgar-Jones) is the reserved, sensible one who buries her feelings for Edward Ferrars (George MacKay) rather than risk showing them. Marianne (Esmé Creed-Miles) is the opposite — she falls hard and fast for the dashing John Willoughby (Frank Dillane), consequences be damned. It's the original "restraint vs. big feelings" sister dynamic, and honestly it still holds up 200+ years later.

Streaming later: No streaming date has been confirmed yet. We'll update this page the moment one drops.

Is It on Netflix?

No — this new Sense and Sensibility is not a Netflix title. It's easy to assume otherwise since so many recent Austen adaptations (Persuasion, the new Pride and Prejudice series) have landed on Netflix, but this one's a theatrical release from Focus Features and Working Title, distributed in theaters first.

If you're after a Sense and Sensibility fix on Netflix in the meantime, the classic 1995 Emma Thompson version does show up there occasionally, though availability shifts by region and isn't guaranteed.

How It Compares to Earlier Versions

For a lot of people, "Sense and Sensibility" means one thing: Ang Lee's 1995 film, with Emma Thompson (who also wrote the Oscar-winning screenplay) as Elinor, Kate Winslet as Marianne, and Hugh Grant as Edward. It's considered one of the best Austen adaptations ever made, so this new version has a genuinely high bar to clear.

There's also a well-loved 2008 BBC miniseries in the mix — three episodes, written by Andrew Davies, with Hattie Morahan and Charity Wakefield as the sisters and Dan Stevens as Edward. Davies has said his goal was to make viewers forget the 1995 film entirely, and the three-episode format gave him room to dig into some of the book's darker, more sensual beats that the movie didn't have time for.

This isn't marketed as a remake — it's simply a new adaptation of Austen's novel (the eighth screen version overall), so comparisons are inevitable but it's very much its own take, with a younger cast and a director coming off the acclaimed "Blue Jean."

About the Novel

Sense and Sensibility was Jane Austen's first published novel, released in 1811 — before Pride and Prejudice, Emma, or any of her other more famous books. It was published anonymously, credited only to "By a Lady," since Austen didn't put her name on her own work during her lifetime.

The story is set in the 1790s, in the tightly-ranked world of Georgian England, where a woman's financial security depended almost entirely on marriage. That pressure is the engine behind everything Elinor and Marianne go through — it's less about romance for its own sake and more about what happens when love and survival are tangled up together.

Why Everyone's Talking About It

Part of it is just timing — this is dropping the same fall as a new "Pride and Prejudice" series, so it's shaping up to be a whole Austen season, and period-drama fans are not going to complain about that.

The trailer's tagline — "no one knows your heart like a sister" — is doing exactly what it's supposed to: hitting the sister-bond crowd right in the chest. If you cried during the Walter Boys sister scenes or anything Summer I Turned Pretty, this is going to get you too.
