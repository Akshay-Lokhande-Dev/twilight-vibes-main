The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth Trailer Is Here

Cliff Booth is back, and honestly, the trailer is exactly the kind of Hollywood chaos we wanted from this movie. Netflix has finally dropped the first full trailer for The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, with Brad Pitt stepping back into the role he played in Once Upon a Time... in Hollywood. Seeing that face again would already be enough to get people talking, but the movie has a much bigger trick up its sleeve.

The story jumps from 1969 to 1977, and Cliff is no longer simply Rick Dalton’s stunt double. He is working as a Hollywood fixer now, which basically means people call him when a problem needs solving and asking too many questions is probably a bad idea. The trailer gives us a grittier Los Angeles, more trouble, more crime and a Cliff who seems very comfortable walking straight into situations most people would run from.

Then there is the absolutely wild combination behind the camera: David Fincher directing a Quentin Tarantino script. That is not the sort of pairing you casually scroll past. Tarantino created Cliff, Fincher gets to put his own visual stamp on the character, and Brad Pitt is caught right in the middle of it all. Suddenly this feels less like a simple follow-up and much more like a movie event.

What Is The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth About?

The movie picks up eight years after Once Upon a Time... in Hollywood, with Cliff trying to make a living in a Los Angeles that has changed quite a bit since we last saw him. He is not tied to Rick Dalton anymore, and his collection of jobs is about as random as you would expect: Hollywood fixer, bartender and even projectionist. The fixer work, though, is what drags him into the mess at the center of the story.

Cliff gets a call from talent agent Karen Crosby, played by Elizabeth Debicki, who needs help with her client, TV star Charlie Peters (Scott Caan). Charlie is the kind of actor who still does his own stunts, which makes his connection with Cliff feel almost too perfect. His wife Toni, played by Carla Gugino, and former football star Roderick “Hot Rod” Harrison, played by Yahya Abdul-Mateen II, are also caught up in the situation.

Things get even messier when the Verdusco brothers, played by Matt Groove and JB Tadena, enter the picture. They are gangsters operating around Los Angeles, and judging from the trailer, they are exactly the sort of people you do not want Cliff having to negotiate with. Put all of that together and you have a pretty good recipe for a very bad week in Cliff Booth’s life.

The simple version: Cliff gets a job helping people in Hollywood, but the job pulls him into a much bigger problem involving crime, secrets and people who are not easy to deal with.

Is The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth a Movie or Series?

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth is a movie, not a TV series, so yes, this is the big-screen return of Cliff Booth rather than another Netflix episodic project. Netflix has Brad Pitt leading the film, with David Fincher directing from Quentin Tarantino’s screenplay. It gets a two-week IMAX run before heading to Netflix, which makes the theatrical release feel like something worth paying attention to even if you eventually plan to watch it at home.

The Adventures of Cliff Booth: The Earlier Title

If you have been searching for The Adventures of Cliff Booth, you are looking for the same Cliff Booth project. That was the title used earlier in the movie's development; the official release title is now The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth.

The name The Adventures of Cliff Booth is also being used for Quentin Tarantino's upcoming Cliff Booth novel, which is scheduled for December 8, 2026.

What Happened to Cliff Booth?

If you remember the first movie, Cliff was mostly known as Rick Dalton's stunt double — driver, fixer, close friend, and quietly the most dangerous person in any room he walked into. Eight years later, the stunt work is gone, and so is Rick. The trailer slips in a small explanation for why: Cliff mentions that Rick got too heavy for him to keep doubling. That's it. One throwaway line that quietly closes the door on their whole working relationship, which is a very Tarantino way of handling something that could have been a whole emotional scene.

What fills the gap is the fixer work, and honestly it suits Cliff far better than standing in for somebody else ever did. The people who need a Hollywood problem solved — quietly, without paperwork — know who to call. When he is not doing that, he is bartending or working a projector at a cinema somewhere. None of it sounds like a man who found his retirement particularly satisfying, which is probably why Karen Crosby's call at the center of the story lands the way it does.

David Fincher and Quentin Tarantino Are Working Together

Let’s talk about the part of this movie that has the internet doing a double take: David Fincher is directing a Quentin Tarantino script. Tarantino is the person who created Cliff Booth in the first place, while Fincher is bringing a completely different filmmaking language to the character. If you are already imagining what a Fincher-shot Los Angeles crime story written by Tarantino might look like, the trailer gives you plenty to play with.

It also helps that Fincher and Brad Pitt have history. They already worked together on Se7en, Fight Club and The Curious Case of Benjamin Button, so this is not their first dance. But seeing Pitt back as Cliff while Fincher directs him in a Tarantino-written story is a very different combination from anything they have done together before.

The trailer leans into that contrast. The Los Angeles we see feels darker and rougher, with plenty of nighttime trouble and the sense that Cliff is getting dragged deeper into situations that are much bigger than a normal Hollywood job. Then Tarantino’s dialogue cuts through it with that familiar loose, character-heavy rhythm. It is a strange combination on paper, and that is exactly why it is so fun to watch.

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth Cast

Brad Pitt is obviously the reason we are here, returning as Cliff Booth, but the new movie surrounds him with a cast that makes his 1977 Hollywood world look considerably messier. Elizabeth Debicki plays talent agent Karen Crosby, the person who brings Cliff into the main problem by asking him to help her client, TV star Charlie Peters, played by Scott Caan.

Charlie is a stunt-happy television star, which feels like a pretty perfect person for Cliff to end up dealing with. His wife Toni, played by Carla Gugino, is also part of the story, while Yahya Abdul-Mateen II plays former football champion and screen star Roderick “Hot Rod” Harrison. These characters all sit inside the Hollywood world Cliff is trying to navigate, so the cast is not just a collection of famous names passing through.

Then the movie throws some gangsters into the mix. Matt Groove and JB Tadena play the Verdusco brothers, while Peter Weller, Corey Fogelmanis and Karren Karagulian round out the wider cast. There is also one returning face from the original: Timothy Olyphant is back as James Stacy, the one-armed actor he played briefly in Once Upon a Time... in Hollywood — a small but welcome piece of connective tissue for people who remember the first film closely.

Is The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth a Sequel?

Technically yes, but it is not a sequel in the way most people use that word. Leonardo DiCaprio is not coming back as Rick Dalton — the film has moved on from that friendship as its engine, which is the right call. Rick and Cliff's dynamic was the heart of Once Upon a Time... in Hollywood, and trying to recreate it eight years later in a different story would have been a mistake.

What you get instead is Cliff as a standalone character, dropped into a 1977 Los Angeles that barely remembers the Hollywood of 1969. Timothy Olyphant does return, reprising his role as James Stacy from the first film — so there are threads back to the original if you want them. But the movie is not built around those connections. It trusts you to bring your own memory of who Cliff is and then gets on with giving him a whole new problem to deal with.

Watching the first film first is still worth doing — not because you will be lost without it, but because Cliff's past clearly still sits somewhere behind his eyes in this one, and knowing where he came from makes certain moments land differently.

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth Release Date

The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth gets its first big-screen moment with a two-week exclusive IMAX run beginning November 25, 2026. That is a pretty serious theatrical push for a Netflix movie, especially when you remember that this is a Fincher movie starring Brad Pitt and written by Tarantino.

After that IMAX run, the movie lands on Netflix on December 23, 2026. So there are basically two dates to circle: November if you want the giant-screen experience, and December if your ideal movie night involves a couch, snacks and absolutely no reason to leave the house.

There Is Also a Cliff Booth Novel

There is another interesting part of this release. Quentin Tarantino is also publishing a new book called The Adventures of Cliff Booth.

The book is scheduled to be published on December 8, 2026, which puts it between the movie's IMAX release and its Netflix release.

Tarantino previously turned Once Upon a Time... in Hollywood into a novel, so this gives fans another way to spend time with Cliff's character outside the movie.

Our Take From the Trailer

The biggest thing I took from the trailer is that this does not look like it is trying to remake the magic of the first movie beat for beat. The original lived inside 1969 Hollywood, with Rick Dalton, Cliff’s friendship with him and Tarantino’s very specific version of Los Angeles. This one jumps to 1977 and gives Cliff a life of his own, which is probably the smarter way to bring the character back.

Cliff is older, he is working alone, and now his ability to deal with dangerous people is literally part of his job. That gives the sequel a reason to exist beyond nostalgia. We get to find out what happened to this guy after his old Hollywood life changed, rather than watching him repeat the same stunt-double routine.

And then there is Fincher. The trailer has a darker, tougher edge, while the dialogue still has that loose Tarantino flavor underneath it. We obviously cannot judge an entire movie from two minutes of footage, but the combination is intriguing enough that I am very curious to see what happens when Fincher gets a full movie to play with Tarantino’s Cliff Booth.

Honestly, that may be the most exciting part of this whole thing: it feels familiar without looking like a carbon copy. That is exactly what I would want from a Cliff Booth follow-up.
