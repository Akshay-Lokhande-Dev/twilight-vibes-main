What Is Artificial About?

Andrew Garfield plays Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI, in a dramatization of the five chaotic days in November 2023 when OpenAI's board abruptly fired Altman — then reinstated him days later after employee backlash and public pressure. If that sounds like a niche tech-industry footnote, the trailer treats it more like a psychological thriller than a boardroom drama, leaning into unsettling, almost horror-adjacent tone rather than a straightforward biopic.

Director Luca Guadagnino has said the film traces roughly two decades of machine-learning research rather than fixating purely on the firing itself, positioning the AI arms race as the real backdrop to the personal drama. In the trailer, a fairly unsettling Garfield-as-Altman narrates the moment ChatGPT was named, while later lines gesture at the scale of what's coming: "Countries will fall" and "Industries are gonna collapse."

Artificial premieres at the New York Film Festival on October 5, 2026, before opening in select US theaters on December 25, 2026, with a wider nationwide expansion following in January 2027. This is a theatrical release through Neon, positioned squarely in awards season.

Is Artificial Based on a True Story?

Yes — it dramatizes real, widely reported events: Sam Altman's sudden November 2023 firing by OpenAI's board and his reinstatement as CEO within days, amid an employee revolt and a signed letter threatening mass resignation. That said, this is a feature film with a screenplay, not a documentary, so expect interpretation and dramatized dialogue rather than a literal recreation. A reporter who claimed to have seen a draft of the script noted that both Musk and Altman would "probably hate their portrayals" — though the version he saw may have changed before filming.

Why Did Amazon MGM Drop It?

An awkward conflict of interest: Artificial was originally set up at Amazon MGM Studios. Amazon later invested a reported $50 billion into OpenAI as part of a major partnership — making it more than a little awkward for Amazon's own studio to release a film that portrays OpenAI's leadership in an unflattering light. Amazon MGM dropped the project earlier in 2026, and Neon picked it up instead, eventually locking in the Christmas Day release.

Why Everyone's Talking About It

Partly because of the cast, partly because of the timing. This is Garfield's second high-profile tech-founder role after The Social Network, and the comparisons to that film have been immediate and constant — critics are already calling this the AI era's answer to it. The film also arrives in a genuinely crowded "billionaire movie" moment: Alex Gibney's Elon Musk documentary just premiered at Venice, and Jeremy Strong is playing Mark Zuckerberg in a separate upcoming film. Artificial is arguably the biggest and most star-studded of the bunch.

There's also the simple fact that this story is still unfolding in real time. Altman and Musk are still publicly feuding as this trailer drops, which makes a dramatized version of their earlier conflict feel less like history and more like current events with better lighting.

We don't usually cover films this far outside our usual lane, but given how much this has been cutting through everywhere this week, it felt worth writing up properly rather than ignoring it. We'll have thoughts after it premieres at NYFF.
