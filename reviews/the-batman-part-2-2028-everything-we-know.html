The Batman: Part II (2028): Release Date, Cast & Everything We Know

Six years, three release dates, and one Winter Soldier turned district attorney later — here's everything actually confirmed about Matt Reeves' long-awaited sequel.

Yes, this is a real update, not a repeat of last year's news. The Batman: Part II has been delayed so many times at this point that half the internet has stopped believing it's a real movie, but there's actually something to report: a new release date, a locked cast, and thirty seconds of genuinely moody camera-test footage that people are still dissecting frame by frame. Here's everything confirmed so far.

What Is The Batman: Part II About?

Details are still tightly guarded, but Matt Reeves has said the sequel picks up after the events of HBO's The Penguin series and pushes further into the corruption themes the first film only started to unpack. "It goes into places that he couldn't anticipate in the first one," Reeves has said, promising the story expands on aspects of Bruce Wayne's character "you never got to see."

The biggest plot thread being tracked by fans is Harvey Dent's arc. Gotham's district attorney is set to undergo the transformation that turns him into Two-Face, and based on the winter imagery in the marketing so far (snow tires on the Batmobile, snowfall in the camera test), this installment looks like it trades the first film's relentless rain for a full-on Gotham winter.

The Batman: Part II (2028)

Release Date & Why It Keeps Moving

The Batman: Part II is currently scheduled for February 18, 2028 — a date that has moved three separate times. It was originally set for October 2025, pushed to October 2026 because of the 2023 Hollywood strikes, moved again to October 2027 for additional writing time, and finally shifted to its current February 2028 slot to give Reeves more room in post-production.

Do the math and it stings a little: by the time this releases, it'll be almost six years since the first film hit theaters in March 2022. DC co-chief James Gunn has defended the gap by pointing out it's not unusual for sequels — seven years between Alien and Aliens, fourteen between the two Incredibles films — but that hasn't stopped fans from joking that Pattinson might need a cane by Part III.

Wait, Sebastian Stan Is Two-Face?

That's the working assumption, though it's worth being precise about what's actually confirmed versus what's implied. Reeves has confirmed Stan is playing Harvey Dent. Stan himself has stayed cryptic about it, only saying he's taking on more than one side of the character, and he's talked about working closely with the hair and makeup team on getting his look right. Neither Stan nor Reeves has said the words "Two-Face" out loud in public. Every trade report reading between those lines has landed on the same conclusion though, and it fits: in the comics, Two-Face's defining trait is the war between Dent's idealistic and criminal halves, which lines up exactly with an actor coyly describing "many roles."

There was a brief window where a different rumor circulated — that Stan might instead be playing Victor Zsasz, a much smaller-scale serial killer character — but Wikipedia's currently maintained cast list and the bulk of recent reporting have settled back on Dent/Two-Face as the confirmed role.

Is It Connected to the Wider DC Universe?

No — and this is worth clarifying because it trips people up. Reeves' Batman films exist under DC's "Elseworlds" banner, a deliberately separate continuity from James Gunn and Peter Safran's rebooted DC Universe (the one with Superman and the rest of the shared-universe films and shows). The Batman: Part II won't reference or be referenced by the mainline DCU, and Pattinson's Batman isn't the same version audiences see anywhere else.

Why People Are Still Talking About It

Partly stubbornness, honestly — after three delays, every scrap of news gets amplified because fans are starving for anything concrete. But the camera test genuinely did its job: a brief, moody glimpse of Pattinson back in costume was enough to send people into a full frame-by-frame breakdown, picking apart everything from the cowl's silhouette to what the weather might hint at for the story. Combined with a locked, stacked cast (an Oscar-winning cinematographer, a Marvel alum stepping into one of Batman's most iconic villains), there's a real sense that this is finally, actually happening.

There's also the simple fact that The Penguin HBO series proved this version of Gotham still has an audience hungry for more, delay or no delay. Whether that patience holds for another eighteen months remains to be seen.

FAQ: The Batman: Part II (2028)

When does The Batman: Part II come out?

The Batman: Part II is scheduled to release in theaters on February 18, 2028, after being delayed three times from its original October 2025 date.

Who plays Two-Face in The Batman: Part II?

Sebastian Stan plays Harvey Dent, Gotham's district attorney, who transforms into Two-Face over the course of the film.

Is Robert Pattinson returning as Batman?

Yes. Robert Pattinson returns as Bruce Wayne/Batman, alongside Jeffrey Wright as Jim Gordon, Andy Serkis as Alfred, and Colin Farrell as Oz Cobb/the Penguin.

Why has The Batman: Part II been delayed so many times?

The film was pushed from October 2025 to October 2026 due to the 2023 Hollywood strikes, then to October 2027 for additional writing time, and finally to February 18, 2028 to give director Matt Reeves more time in post-production.

Is The Batman: Part II connected to the DC Universe?

No. It's part of Matt Reeves' separate Batman Epic Crime Saga, labeled a DC Elseworlds project, and exists outside James Gunn and Peter Safran's rebooted DC Universe continuity.

Does The Batman: Part II have a trailer yet?

Not a full trailer as of this writing. Director Matt Reeves has released a roughly 30-second camera test showing Robert Pattinson in the Batsuit amid rain and flashing police lights, but no official teaser trailer has dropped yet.
