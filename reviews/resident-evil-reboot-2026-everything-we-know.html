Resident Evil Reboot (2026): Release Date, Cast & Everything We Know

The franchise has failed at this twice already. This time it's got the director of Barbarian and Weapons — and he's never seen a single Resident Evil movie.

Resident Evil has had a rough time on the big screen. Six live-action movies, one prequel spinoff, one Netflix series — and somehow none of them ever really captured what made the games work in the first place. So when Sony announced yet another reboot, the reaction from a lot of longtime fans was closer to exhaustion than excitement.

Then the name attached to it changed everything: Zach Cregger, the director behind Barbarian and this year's Weapons, two of the most talked-about horror films in recent memory. Suddenly a franchise that had become something of a punchline had a director capable of actually delivering the dread the games are known for.

The new Resident Evil hits US theaters and IMAX on September 18, 2026 — here's everything confirmed so far.

Why This Reboot Exists At All

To understand why Sony went back to the drawing board yet again, it helps to remember how badly the last two attempts landed. Resident Evil: Welcome to Raccoon City (2021) was meant to be a faithful, back-to-basics adaptation after the Milla Jovovich series wrapped up — and it landed with a thud, critically and commercially. Netflix tried its own live-action series the following year and cancelled it after a single season.

Constantin Film, which has held onto the movie rights for over two decades at this point, started planning another reboot in early 2022. It took until January 2025 for Cregger to actually get attached — and once he did, four different studios reportedly went into a bidding war for the film, including Netflix and Warner Bros. Sony ended up winning, bringing PlayStation Productions on board as well, which makes sense given how directly the film leans on the games rather than the earlier movies.

Cregger Has Never Seen a Resident Evil Movie — On Purpose

This is the detail that's gotten the most attention from fans, and it's a genuinely unusual approach for a reboot: Cregger has said flatly that he's never watched any of the previous Resident Evil films. Not one.

What he has done is put in what he's described as thousands of hours with the games themselves. Talking to Double Toasted, he explained that the new film "probably lives more in the world of [Resident Evil] 2 and 3," but leans closer to the tone of Resident Evil 4 — widely considered the point where the series shifted from pure survival horror into something more action-driven, while never losing its nastiest instincts.

In his own words: "If there are people out there that are just rabid fans of the movie franchise, they're probably not really prepared for what I'm going to be doing," Cregger told Deadline. "But I think the people that are fans of the games are probably going to be stoked."

That's either a reassuring signal or a mild warning, depending on which camp you fall into.

The Story: A Courier, A Package, One Very Bad Night

Unlike every previous Resident Evil film, which leaned on ensemble casts bouncing between multiple perspectives, this one is staying tight and personal. The story follows Bryan (Austin Abrams), a medical courier whose job is ferrying organs between hospitals for transplant operations. On what starts as a routine delivery run into Raccoon City, everything falls apart around him in the space of a single night, and he has to fight his way through a city collapsing into a viral outbreak just to survive it.

It's a much smaller, more contained setup than fans might expect from a franchise usually built around military units and corporate conspiracies, but that scale fits both Cregger's filmography and the tone he's described wanting to hit. Barbarian and Weapons both work by trapping ordinary people in situations that spiral out of control fast — and a single courier stumbling into a zombie outbreak on his way to make a delivery fits that instinct perfectly.

The film is set in a modern-day version of Raccoon City, with a story that reportedly runs parallel to the events of the 1998 game Resident Evil 2, though it isn't a direct adaptation of it or of any of the eleven films that came before.

Behind the Camera

Filming began in October 2025 in Prague, wrapped in time for editing to start that February, and the whole production has moved fast enough that the film is hitting theaters less than a year after cameras first rolled. Cregger co-wrote the script with Shay Hatten, whose credits include John Wick: Chapter 4 and Army of the Dead — a background that lines up well with a story that needs to balance genuine scares with some hard action.

The first teaser dropped on April 30, 2026, with a fuller trailer following on July 23. Both leaned hard into practical-feeling creature design and the kind of sustained, close-quarters dread Cregger's become known for, rather than the more CGI-heavy spectacle of earlier entries in the franchise.

The film has landed an R rating in the US for "strong bloody violence, gore and language throughout" — which, if Barbarian and Weapons are anything to go by, is not a rating Cregger is interested in softening.

It's fair to be cautious about any Resident Evil movie at this point — the franchise has earned that skepticism the hard way. But the combination of factors here is genuinely different from what came before it: a director with two back-to-back horror hits, a studio that actually owns the gaming side of the IP working alongside the film side, and a filmmaker who's explicitly building from the games instead of trying to patch up the mistakes of the last six movies. Whether that translates into the film longtime fans have been waiting for is still an open question — but for the first time in a long time, Resident Evil has a genuine reason to be taken seriously again.

FAQ: Resident Evil Reboot (2026)

When does the new Resident Evil movie come out?

The Resident Evil reboot opens in US theaters and IMAX on September 18, 2026.

Is the new Resident Evil a reboot?

Yes. It's the second attempt at rebooting the live-action film franchise, following Resident Evil: Welcome to Raccoon City (2021) and Netflix's short-lived 2022 TV series.

Who directs the new Resident Evil movie?

Zach Cregger, director of Barbarian and Weapons, directs and co-wrote the film with Shay Hatten.

Has Zach Cregger seen the previous Resident Evil movies?

No. Cregger has said he has never watched any of the previous Resident Evil films and is building the reboot from the video games instead.

Who stars in the Resident Evil reboot?

Austin Abrams stars as Bryan, a medical courier, alongside Paul Walter Hauser, Zach Cherry, Kali Reis, and Johnno Wilson.

What is the Resident Evil reboot rated?

It has been rated R in the US for strong bloody violence, gore, and language throughout.
