10 Best Romantic Movies on Netflix a Twilight Fan Must Watch

For the people who didn't stay for the vampires. You stayed for the longing.

Home  ›  Lists  ›  Romantic Movies on Netflix for Twilight Fans

By Twilight Vibes · Updated September 2026

Most "movies like Twilight" lists go looking for vampires. They hand you a stack of supernatural teen romances and call it a day, as if the fangs were the point.

They weren't. What kept people rewatching was the wanting. The held breath. Two people orbiting each other in a permanently overcast town, every conversation loaded, nobody able to just say the thing. That feeling is still driving the Twilight renaissance two decades on, and you don't need a coven to find it.

So this list is built around that instead. Some of these are teen romances. One's a Nora Ephron classic. One is about a 1950s dressmaker and will annoy you if you're in a light mood. What they share is atmosphere, chemistry with friction in it, and the ache that made you sit through New Moon more than once.

About Netflix availability: Every title here was confirmed on Netflix as of September 2026, but catalogues shift constantly and differ a lot country to country. Netflix Originals (marked below) are the safest bets internationally. Licensed titles, meaning the older non-Netflix-produced films, are the ones most likely to be missing where you are. Worth a quick search before you commit to movie night.

1. Through My Window (2022)

2022Netflix OriginalSpanishTeen romance

Raquel has spent years quietly obsessed with Ares, the impossibly wealthy boy who moved in next door. He's aloof. His family thinks she's beneath them. She watches him through her bedroom window, which the film treats as significantly less unhinged than it sounds.

Why a Twilight fan may like it: Of everything on this list, this is the closest structural match. Ordinary girl, unreachable boy, a family that disapproves, and a romance built almost entirely out of stolen glances before anyone speaks a full sentence to each other. If you've ever tried to describe the Bella-and-Edward dynamic to someone who hasn't seen Twilight, you could honestly just play them this instead.

The dynamic: Push-pull, and heavily weighted toward the pull. Ares runs hot and cold in a way that will either read as delicious tension or as a red flag, depending entirely on your tolerance for brooding rich boys who won't text back.

The vibe: Barcelona, glossy and warm. The one big departure from Forks. Less rain, more balconies.

Twilight Vibes verdict

It's not a great film and it isn't trying to be. It's a mood delivery system, and on that front it works. Start here if you want the fastest possible route back to that feeling — just know the sequels drop off hard.

2. 20th Century Girl (2022)

2022Netflix OriginalKoreanComing-of-age

1999, South Korea. Bo-ra promises her best friend she'll gather intel on a boy while the friend is abroad for heart surgery. What follows is a first-love story told with a camcorder, a landline, and an almost unbearable amount of restraint.

Why a Twilight fan may like it: Because you're not actually here for happy endings. You're here for the ache, and this film is engineered to find it. It understands something Twilight understood too — that the most devastating romantic moments are usually the ones where nothing gets said out loud.

The dynamic: Slow-burn in the truest sense. It runs on mistaken identity and bad timing, not conflict. Nobody in this movie is a villain, and that's part of what makes it hurt.

The vibe: Nostalgic and sun-faded, all school hallways and analog technology. Gentle for about ninety minutes, then merciless.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Best film on this list. Don't put it on if you have anywhere to be afterwards. Block out the evening, keep something comforting nearby, and accept that you will be useless for an hour once it ends.

3. About Time (2013)

2013Licensed titleBritishRomantic drama

Tim learns on his twenty-first birthday that the men in his family can travel back through their own lives. He immediately decides to use this power for the least ambitious purpose imaginable: getting a girlfriend.

Why a Twilight fan may like it: Twilight was always partly about immortality, about what it means to love someone when time works differently for the two of you. About Time takes that same question and strips out the supernatural drama entirely, which somehow makes it hit harder. The romance is lovely. The last act is about a father and son, and it will take you apart.

The dynamic: Warm and unusually functional. These two like each other, which is rarer in romance films than it should be. The tension comes from time itself, not from anyone behaving badly.

The vibe: Rainy London, a house by the Cornish sea, richard-curtis-shaped coziness throughout.

Twilight Vibes verdict

They sold it as a rom-com. It's a film about mortality wearing a rom-com's coat, and the last twenty minutes are not about the romance at all. Go in knowing that and it's the most rewarding watch here.

4. The Half of It (2020)

2020Netflix OriginalUSComing-of-age

Ellie Chu writes other students' essays for cash in a small, dull town she's desperate to leave. A well-meaning jock hires her to write love letters to a girl — and Ellie, who has her own feelings about that girl, says yes anyway.

Why a Twilight fan may like it: The small-town claustrophobia is exact: stuck somewhere grey and rain-soaked with nobody who quite gets you. And it's fundamentally about longing expressed through writing, which is the most Bella Swan thing imaginable.

The dynamic: A love triangle, but a thoughtful one. It's less interested in who wins than in what each person wants the other to see in them, and the friendship at its centre ends up being the most moving relationship in the film.

The vibe: Quiet, bookish, faintly melancholy. Lots of overcast skies and long walks.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Smarter and sadder than the Netflix teen-romance shelf usually allows. If you've been burned by generic YA adaptations, this is the one that'll win you back.

5. To All the Boys I've Loved Before (2018)

2018Netflix OriginalUSRom-com

Lara Jean writes letters to every boy she's ever loved and hides them in a hatbox. They get mailed. Chaos, and then a fake-dating arrangement, follows.

This is the comfort watch of the list. Butterflies, no emotional damage. It also does something Twilight did well and rarely gets credit for, which is putting an introverted, slightly awkward girl at the romantic centre of the story without making her the punchline.

Fake dating slides into real feelings, handled with charm. Peter Kavinsky became a generational crush for a reason, and the reason is that he's kind. Refreshing if the brooding-boy archetype has started wearing thin. The look of it is bright and cosy, all Portland-adjacent Pacific Northwest, which Twilight fans will find familiar in a nice way.

Twilight Vibes verdict

The lightest thing here and unapologetic about it. Two sequels follow if you get attached, though the first is comfortably the strongest.

6. Phantom Thread (2017)

2017Licensed titleUS/UKPeriod drama

A meticulous, controlling London dressmaker in the 1950s meets a young waitress and brings her into his very closed world. She does not stay in the role he's designed for her.

Why a Twilight fan may like it: This is the entry for anyone whose favourite part of Twilight was the danger, the sense that this love might consume someone. Phantom Thread is a romance about obsession and control and two people locked in a power struggle they both secretly enjoy. Gorgeous, strange, and much darker than the costumes let on.

The dynamic: Toxic, and the film knows it. What makes it remarkable is that it refuses to be a cautionary tale. It's a study of two people finding the exact wrong person to be perfect for. Deeply uncomfortable. Weirdly romantic.

The vibe: Hushed and immaculate. Rustling fabric, muted colours, a house where everyone whispers.

Twilight Vibes verdict

The hardest watch on this list and the most rewarding if you're in the mood. Not a casual movie-night pick — save it for when you want something to chew on.

7. Cruel Intentions (1999)

1999Licensed titleUSTeen drama

Two wealthy Manhattan step-siblings make a bet involving the seduction of a headmaster's daughter. It goes exactly as badly as you'd expect, and then worse.

Why a Twilight fan may like it: Because it's the other side of the romantic coin — love as a weapon rather than a refuge. It also captures something the Twilight films had in spades: complete, unembarrassed commitment to melodrama. Nobody in this movie underplays anything, and that's the appeal.

How it plays: Manipulative, then unexpectedly sincere, and the shift between those modes is what makes it stick. Ryan Phillippe's character falling for real is the engine of the whole film. All of it wrapped in late-90s opulence: marble staircases, cigarettes, a soundtrack that still holds up. Dated in places, gleefully so in others.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Not a good influence on anyone, and not really trying to be. It's a time capsule with real bite, and if you've never seen it, the ending still lands.

8. Voicemails for Isabelle (2026)

2026Netflix OriginalEnglishRomantic comedy

Jill's sister Isabelle dies, and Jill keeps calling her number anyway. Long, oversharing, three-in-the-morning voicemails about her collapsing life as a pastry chef in San Francisco. What she doesn't know is that the number has been reassigned to Wes, a real estate agent in Austin, who listens to every one of them and falls for her.

Why a Twilight fan may like it: It's a romance built entirely on imagination. Wes loves a woman he has never seen, assembled out of her voice and her worst days. That's the Bella-and-Edward engine with the supernatural stripped out. The film is about wanting someone you have no right to want, and knowing it.

The dynamic: Grief first, romance second. Zoey Deutch plays Jill funny and raw at the same time, which is harder than it looks, and the film never lets the comedy off the hook for the sadness underneath it.

The vibe: Warm kitchens, bad dates, a phone lighting up in the dark. It sat at 88% on Rotten Tomatoes across 58 reviews, which is the best any Netflix romance managed this year.

9. People We Meet on Vacation (2026)

2026Netflix OriginalEnglishRom-com

Two best friends who take one trip together every summer, across a decade, until something breaks the pattern. Adapted from Emily Henry's novel, and nominated for the Outstanding Television Movie Emmy.

Friends-to-lovers is the slow burn in its purest form: years of proximity and nothing said. If the part of Twilight that got you was the stretch before anything happened, this structure is built out of that tension and nothing else. The timeline jumps between trips, so you spend the whole film watching two people almost get there and then not.

It's sun-drenched and travel-brochure pretty, the least moody thing here by a wide margin. Save it for when you want the opposite of rain.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Comfortable rather than surprising, and enjoyable if that's what you came for. Emily Henry fans will have opinions about the adaptation; everyone else will have a nice time.

10. Sleepless in Seattle (1993)

1993Licensed titleUSClassic rom-com

A widower's young son calls a radio show to talk about his dad. A journalist across the country hears it and can't stop thinking about the voice. The two leads spend almost the entire film apart.

Why a Twilight fan may like it: Two reasons. First, the Pacific Northwest setting. Seattle is a real Twilight-adjacent destination, roughly four hours from Forks, and this film is soaked in it. Second, and more importantly: it's the purest study of longing in mainstream cinema. An entire romance sustained on wanting, with barely any contact at all.

The dynamic: Almost entirely imagined, which is the point. They're not falling for each other so much as for the idea of each other, and the film is smart enough to know that's both romantic and a little foolish.

The vibe: Rain on windows, houseboats, a soundtrack of standards. Cosy in the specific way that only early-90s films manage.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Thirty-plus years old and still the template everyone's copying. If you're building a rainy-PNW watchlist, this belongs on it, and it pairs unreasonably well with a Forks road trip playlist.

If You Only Watch One

Depends what you're in the mood for, so, the short version:

  • Closest to Twilight's exact energy: Through My Window
  • Want to cry: 20th Century Girl
  • Want comfort, no damage: To All the Boys I've Loved Before

And if you're deep enough in the rewatch spiral that you're considering going back to the source material yet again — no judgement, we've all been there. Our guide to the correct Twilight watch order is there when you need it, and if you're trying to settle a very old argument in the meantime, the Team Edward vs Team Jacob case still holds up.

Common Questions

What romantic movie should I watch after Twilight?

Through My Window is the closest tonal match: same brooding-boy-next-door structure, same unspoken-tension pacing. If what you want is the emotional gut-punch, go with 20th Century Girl instead.

Is the Twilight Saga itself on Netflix?

Twilight's streaming home moves around regularly and differs by country, so it's worth checking rather than assuming. Our guide to where to watch the Twilight movies covers the current situation.

Are all of these on Netflix in every country?

No. Netflix licenses region by region. The Netflix Originals on this list (Through My Window, 20th Century Girl, The Half of It, To All the Boys, Voicemails for Isabelle, People We Meet on Vacation) are the most reliably available worldwide. The licensed titles (About Time, Phantom Thread, Cruel Intentions, Sleepless in Seattle) vary much more and rotate in and out.

Which one is the saddest?

20th Century Girl, comfortably. About Time is a close second, though it earns its tears differently: less tragedy, more the plain fact of time passing.
