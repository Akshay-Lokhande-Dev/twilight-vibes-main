10 Best Romantic Movies on Netflix a Twilight Fan Must Watch

For the people who didn't stay for the vampires. You stayed for the longing.

Home  ›  Lists  ›  Romantic Movies on Netflix for Twilight Fans

By Twilight Vibes · Updated September 2026

Most "movies like Twilight" lists go looking for vampires. They hand you a stack of supernatural teen romances and call it a day, as if the fangs were the point.

They weren't. What kept people rewatching was the wanting. The held breath. Two people orbiting each other in a permanently overcast town, every conversation loaded, nobody able to just say the thing. That feeling is still driving the Twilight renaissance two decades on, and you don't need a coven to find it.

So this list is built around that instead. Some of these are teen romances. One's a Nora Ephron classic. One is about a 1950s dressmaker and will annoy you if you're in a light mood. What they share is atmosphere, chemistry with friction in it, and the ache that made you sit through New Moon more than once.

About Netflix availability: Every title here was confirmed on Netflix as of September 2026, but catalogues shift constantly and differ a lot country to country. Netflix Originals (marked below) are the safest bets internationally. Licensed titles, meaning the older non-Netflix-produced films, are the ones most likely to be missing where you are. Worth a quick search before you commit to movie night.

1. Through My Window (2022)

2022Netflix OriginalSpanishTeen romance

Raquel's been quietly obsessed with Ares for years. He's the impossibly wealthy boy next door who barely registers she exists. His family thinks she's beneath them. She watches him through her bedroom window, and somehow the film makes that feel almost normal.

Of everything on this list, this one copies Twilight's actual blueprint the closest. Ordinary girl, unreachable boy, a family that disapproves, a romance that runs almost entirely on stolen glances before either of them manages a full sentence. Honestly, if a friend's never seen Twilight and you're trying to explain the Bella-Edward thing, just put this on instead.

Ares runs hot and cold in a way that'll either feel delicious or set off every red flag you own, depending on your patience for rich boys who don't text back. Setting-wise it's the one real departure from Forks — Barcelona, sun-drenched, way more balconies than rain.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Not a great film, but it isn't chasing that. It's mood on tap, and it delivers. Good starting point if you want that feeling back fast. Just don't expect the sequels to hold up.

2. 20th Century Girl (2022)

2022Netflix OriginalKoreanComing-of-age

1999, South Korea. Bo-ra promises her best friend she'll scout out a boy while the friend's abroad for heart surgery. What follows is a first-love story told mostly through a camcorder and a landline, with an almost cruel amount of restraint.

You're not really here for happy endings, if we're honest, and you're here for the ache. This one finds it precisely, every time. It gets what Twilight got too: the moments that hurt most are usually the ones where nobody says anything out loud.

It's a proper slow burn, built on mistaken identity and bad timing rather than any real villain, which somehow makes it worse. Everything's sun-faded and nostalgic, school hallways and old technology, gentle for most of its runtime before it turns merciless in the last twenty minutes.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Best film on this list, no contest. Don't start it if you have plans afterward. Clear the evening, keep a snack close, and accept you'll be useless for an hour once the credits roll.

3. About Time (2013)

2013Licensed titleBritishRomantic drama

Tim finds out on his twenty-first birthday that the men in his family can travel back through their own lives. Naturally, the first thing he does with that power is try to get a girlfriend.

Twilight always had this undercurrent about immortality: what loving someone means when time doesn't move the same way for both of you. About Time asks that same question minus the vampires, and somehow it lands harder for it. The romance itself is sweet enough, but the last act turns into something about a father and son that'll wreck you a little.

The relationship here is warm and, unusually for the genre, actually functional: these two just like each other. The tension comes from time itself, not from either of them behaving badly. Rainy London, a house by the Cornish coast, that particular Richard Curtis coziness running through the whole thing.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Marketed as a rom-com; really a film about mortality wearing a rom-com's clothes, and the final twenty minutes have almost nothing to do with the romance. Go in knowing that, and it's probably the most rewarding watch on this whole list.

4. The Half of It (2020)

2020Netflix OriginalUSComing-of-age

Ellie Chu ghostwrites other students' essays for cash, stuck in a small dull town she can't wait to leave. A well-meaning jock pays her to write love letters to a girl he likes, and Ellie, who happens to have her own feelings about that girl, takes the job anyway.

The small-town claustrophobia is spot on: grey, rain-soaked, nobody around who actually gets you. And underneath it all, this is a movie about longing said through writing, which is about as Bella Swan as it gets.

It's a love triangle, but a fairly thoughtful one, less about who wins and more about what each person wants to be seen as. The friendship at its centre ends up mattering more than either romance. Quiet, bookish, a little melancholy, lots of overcast sky and long walks.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Sharper and sadder than most of what Netflix's teen-romance shelf usually offers. If generic YA adaptations have burned you before, this is the one that wins you back.

Official trailer

5. To All the Boys I've Loved Before (2018)

2018Netflix OriginalUSRom-com

Lara Jean writes letters to every boy she's ever loved and stashes them in a hatbox, never meant to be sent. They get mailed anyway. Chaos follows, then a fake-dating arrangement, then the usual complications.

This is the comfort watch on the list: butterflies, zero emotional damage. It also does something Twilight quietly did well and rarely got credit for: putting an introverted, slightly awkward girl at the centre of a romance without turning her into the joke.

Fake dating tips over into real feelings, handled with a light hand throughout. Peter Kavinsky earned his generational-crush status for a simple reason: he's kind. A nice break if the brooding-boy archetype has worn thin lately. Visually it's bright and cosy, Pacific Northwest-adjacent, which will feel familiar to anyone coming from Forks.

Twilight Vibes verdict

The lightest thing on this list, and it knows it. Two sequels if you get attached, though the first one's easily the strongest of the three.

Official trailer

6. Phantom Thread (2017)

2017Licensed titleUS/UKPeriod drama

A meticulous, controlling dressmaker in 1950s London meets a young waitress and pulls her into his tightly closed-off world. She refuses to stay in the role he's built for her.

This one's for anyone whose favourite part of Twilight was the danger: that sense a love like this could actually consume someone. Phantom Thread is about obsession and control, two people locked in a power struggle they both, in some twisted way, enjoy. Gorgeous, strange, far darker than the costumes let on.

It's toxic, and it never once pretends otherwise. This was never going to be a cautionary tale. What it's really watching is two people who found the exact wrong person to be right for. Uncomfortable to sit through. Weirdly, still romantic. Hushed, immaculate, muted colours, a house where everyone speaks in whispers.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Hardest watch on this list, and the most rewarding if you're in the right mood for it. Not a casual movie-night pick. Save it for when you actually want something to chew on.

Official trailer

7. Cruel Intentions (1999)

1999Licensed titleUSTeen drama

Two wealthy Manhattan step-siblings make a bet over seducing their headmaster's daughter. It goes exactly as badly as you'd guess — then worse.

This is the flip side of the romantic coin: love used as a weapon instead of a refuge. It also has something the Twilight films had plenty of — total, unembarrassed commitment to melodrama. Nobody here underplays a single thing, and honestly, that's the appeal.

It plays manipulative first, then unexpectedly sincere, and that shift is really the whole engine of the film. Ryan Phillippe's character actually falling for real is what drives it. Wrapped in late-90s excess throughout: marble staircases, cigarettes, a soundtrack that's aged better than it should have. Dated in spots, gleefully so in others.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Not a good influence on anyone, and it isn't pretending otherwise. A time capsule with real teeth. If you've somehow never seen it, the ending still lands.

8. Voicemails for Isabelle (2026)

2026Netflix OriginalEnglishRomantic comedy

Jill's sister Isabelle dies, and Jill keeps calling her number anyway — long, oversharing, 3 a.m. voicemails about her collapsing life as a pastry chef in San Francisco. What she doesn't know: the number's been reassigned to Wes, a real estate agent in Austin, who listens to every single one and falls for her.

It's a romance built entirely on imagination. Wes falls for a woman he's never seen, pieced together out of her voice and her worst days. That's the Bella-and-Edward setup with the fangs removed: wanting someone you have no real claim to, and knowing it the whole time.

Grief comes first here, romance second. Zoey Deutch plays Jill funny and raw at once, which is trickier than it looks, and the film never lets the jokes off the hook for the sadness sitting underneath them. Warm kitchens, bad dates, a phone lighting up in the dark. Sat at 88% on Rotten Tomatoes across 58 reviews — the best any Netflix romance managed this year.

9. People We Meet on Vacation (2026)

2026Netflix OriginalEnglishRom-com

Official trailer

Two best friends take one trip together every summer for a decade — until something finally breaks the pattern. Adapted from Emily Henry's novel, and it picked up an Outstanding Television Movie Emmy nomination.

Friends-to-lovers doesn't get slower-burn than this: years of proximity, nothing ever said out loud. If the part of Twilight that hooked you was everything before anything actually happened, this is built out of that exact stretch and not much else. The timeline hops between trips, so you spend the whole runtime watching two people almost get there, again and again, and not.

Sun-drenched, travel-brochure pretty. Easily the least moody thing on this list. Save it for a night you want the opposite of rain.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Official trailer

Comfortable more than surprising, and that's fine if that's what you came for. Emily Henry readers will have opinions about the adaptation; everyone else will just have a nice time.

10. Sleepless in Seattle (1993)

1993Licensed titleUSClassic rom-com

A widower's young son calls a radio show to talk about his dad. A journalist across the country hears the broadcast and can't shake the voice. The two leads barely share the same room for the whole film.

Two reasons this one belongs here. First, the Pacific Northwest setting — Seattle's a real Twilight-adjacent destination, about four hours from Forks, and the film is soaked in that same coastal grey. Second, and this is the bigger one: it might be the purest study of longing mainstream cinema has produced, a whole romance held up on wanting, with barely any actual contact.

It's almost entirely imagined, and that's the point: they're not really falling for each other so much as for the idea of each other, and the film's smart enough to know that's both romantic and a little foolish. Rain on windows, houseboats, a soundtrack full of standards. Cosy in that specific way only early-90s films manage.

Twilight Vibes verdict

Over thirty years old and still the template everyone else keeps copying. Belongs on any rainy-PNW watchlist, and it pairs unreasonably well with a Forks road trip playlist.

If You Only Watch One

Depends on the mood, honestly. Quick version:

  • Closest to Twilight's exact energy: Through My Window
  • Want to cry: 20th Century Girl
  • Want comfort, no damage: To All the Boys I've Loved Before

And if you're deep enough into the rewatch spiral that you're seriously considering going back to the source material again — no judgement, we've all been there. Common Questions

What romantic movie should I watch after Twilight?

Through My Window is your closest match — same brooding-boy-next-door setup, same slow unspoken-tension pacing. If you're actually chasing that emotional gut-punch, go with 20th Century Girl instead.

Is the Twilight Saga itself on Netflix?

It moves around a fair bit and depends on your country, so it's worth checking rather than assuming. Our guide to where to watch the Twilight movies has the current picture.

Are all of these on Netflix in every country?

No. Netflix licenses region by region. The Netflix Originals here (Through My Window, 20th Century Girl, The Half of It, To All the Boys, Voicemails for Isabelle, People We Meet on Vacation) are your safest bets worldwide. The licensed titles (About Time, Phantom Thread, Cruel Intentions, Sleepless in Seattle) rotate in and out far more.

Which one is the saddest?

20th Century Girl, no real competition. About Time comes close, but it earns its tears differently: less tragedy, more just the plain fact of time passing.
